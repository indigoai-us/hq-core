Fixed (hooks, stock macOS): the hook runner no longer starts perl to read the clock, and starts it at most once per hook instead of three or four times. On a Mac with the system Bash 3.2 and no coreutils `timeout`, `master-hook.sh` read its millisecond clock with `perl -MTime::HiRes` before and after every child hook and wrapped each child in a perl timeout runner, and `hqd-hook-shim.sh` started a separate perl to probe its modules before starting its client. That was about 26 perl launches per PreToolUse, 49 per UserPromptSubmit and 86 per SessionStart; with several sessions open a reporter measured over a thousand perl launches a minute and linked that rate to repeated macOS watchdog panics. Shells without `EPOCHREALTIME` now use the process-free seconds clock (journal timings keep whole seconds there), Homebrew's `gtimeout` is accepted as the per-child timeout runner, and the shim's client checks its own modules and marks that it started, so one shim call starts one perl. Measured on stock-macOS conditions (/bin/bash 3.2, no timeout on PATH): PreToolUse 26 to 8 perl launches, UserPromptSubmit 49 to 15, SessionStart 86 to 32; with `gtimeout` on PATH the dispatcher starts none. Tests: `master-hook-stock-macos-perl-budget.test.sh` runs a real dispatch with the legacy clock and a PATH without `timeout`, and fails on any clock perl, on more than one perl per child, or on any perl when only `gtimeout` is present; `hqd-hook-shim.test.sh` checks that one shim call starts perl once.
